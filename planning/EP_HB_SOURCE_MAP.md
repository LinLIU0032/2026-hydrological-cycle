## SOURCE_CONFLICT

### SOURCE_CONFLICT 1

Topic: EP water-reservoir residence times  
Source A: EP Interlude F, Table F.3, PDF p. 614 / printed p. 579  
Source B: EP Interlude F narrative, PDF p. 617 / printed p. 582  
Difference: Table F.3 lists oceans/inland seas 3,000–3,500 years, freshwater lakes 50–100 years, rivers/streams 2–6 months, and shallow groundwater 100–200 years. The following narrative gives oceans `4,000 years or less`, lakes/ponds `10 years or less`, rivers `two weeks or less`, and groundwater `two weeks to 10,000 years`.  
Possible reason: the table and narrative use different averaging scopes or source conventions, but the text does not explain the mismatch.  
Impact on teaching: Slide 08 could silently present conflicting values if EP is used directly.  
Recommended option: use primary UE/CN values after cross-source reconciliation; if EP Table F.3 is retained, label values as approximate ranges and do not combine them with the narrative values.  
Need user decision: NO, unless primary-source reconciliation leaves a conflict that changes Slide 08.

### SOURCE_CONFLICT 2

Topic: Solar brightening over Earth history  
Source A: HB Ch.9 opening, PDF p. 218 (`brightened by about 35%`)  
Source B: HB Ch.9, PDF pp. 233 and 254 (`39%`); EP Ch.23, PDF p. 889 / printed p. 854 (`about 30% brighter today`)  
Difference: 30%, 35%, and 39% are all stated for broadly the same long-term solar-luminosity change.  
Possible reason: different reference ages, stellar models, and rounding conventions.  
Impact on teaching: none if Lecture 16 uses only the qualitative faint-young-Sun/thermostat idea; it becomes material if a single percentage is placed on a slide.  
Recommended option: do not teach a percentage in Lecture 16; retain the qualitative statement that solar luminosity increased while liquid water persisted.  
Need user decision: NO.

### SOURCE_CONFLICT 3

Topic: Oxygen-isotope sign of continental precipitation/ice  
Source A: HB Ch.9, PDF p. 223 says rainwater falling on continents is isotopically `heavy`, with a higher 18O/16O ratio than seawater and positive δ18O  
Source B: HB Ch.18, PDF pp. 457–459 says polar ice has an 18O/16O ratio 35‰ lower than seawater and that growing ice sheets remove low-18O water from the ocean

Possible reason: likely wording/sign error or an unstated distinction between water and altered rock in the Ch.9 passage.  
Impact on teaching: could misteach isotope fractionation; isotope derivation is not required by the locked Blueprint.  
Recommended option: classify detailed isotope treatment as OUT OF SCOPE and do not use the Ch.9 sign statement.
